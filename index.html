I am a PhD candidate at the University of St Andrews, affiliated with the PeaceRep (MEND) project, under the supervision of Nicholas Barnes and Elisa D’Amico. In 2026–27, I am a stipendiary Graduate Fellow at the Program on Negotiation at Harvard University, and the Teaching Fellow associated with Professor Robert Mnookin's) seminar How Wars End.

My research sits at the intersection of contentious politics and environmental politics. The first strand examines how states securitise dissent, showing that policing institutions selectively decline to treat protest as a security threat depending on protesters' partisanship. The second examines how climate policy is made, tracing how policy ideas take shape inside EU institutions. My work with Elisa D'Amico brings the two together, asking whether environmental protest moves legislatures across six Western democracies. Across all of this, I use computational and text-as-data methods, including NLP, transformer models, and LLM-based classification. More recently, I have turned to multimodal data, combining text with remote-sensing data from LiDAR and UAV drone surveys to assess whether environmental policies deliver on the ground.
